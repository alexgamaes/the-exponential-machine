## 2. The new mechanic on screen
Three candidates, judged on: can a new player read cause → effect in one glance, is there a live decision, does it
feel different from Stage 2's "Sell 0–75%" switch, and does it fit the existing panels without moving anything.

| | Candidate | On screen | Clear? | Fun? | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| A | **A price you set**, with a demand curve | 3-button price switch + "about N buyers a second at this price" | Medium: a curve is abstract; players set it once and leave it. | Low after the first minute. Looks like Stage 2's switch, so it reads as a repeat. | Use only as the control for C. |
| B | **Software that sells beside hardware** | A second product row, its own money line | High, but it is "another producer that makes money". No new decision. | Low: more of the same. | As idea cards (e.g. "A spreadsheet": home computers sell twice as fast). Software is Stage 4's mechanic. |
| C | **Users and word of mouth** | A Users number in the panel, with "+N from word of mouth" growing beside it | High: the number visibly snowballs. Ties straight to the goal (a computer in every home). | High: the first real compounding the player watches, which is the game's theme. | **Pick, with A's price as its lever.** |

**Picked: you no longer own the machines. You make computers and the world buys them.**
Each computer sold goes to a home and keeps running there; its operations count as yours (speed, total, goal).
Your garage makes computers; buyers take them at your price; every owner tells friends (word of mouth = more buyers).
The decision: **cheap** = more buyers, users grow faster, little money; **dear** = money for factories, slower spread.
It differs from Stage 2: there you split a flow you already had; here you choose growth now vs. money for later supply.
(Numbers are for systems; the UX only needs the three states below to exist and be visible.)

Panel **"Sell computers"** (same slot as Stage 2's "Sell computer time", under "Your garage"):
```
SELL COMPUTERS
 Price  [ $400 ][ $1,200 ][ $3,000 ]                    ← same .seg widget, numbers not %
 At $1,200: about 30 buyers a second, $36,000 a second.  ← forecast for the chosen price, updated once a second
 Users: 4,120   +30 a second · +12 from word of mouth    ← word-of-mouth part bold, the only bold number
 Buyers are waiting: build more computers.               ← one status line, one of three texts
```
- **Teaching without a tutorial:** the forecast line reads the consequence before you commit; hovering/focusing a price
  button swaps the forecast to that price (no pop-up, same line, same height). The status line names the bottleneck:
  "Buyers are waiting: build more computers." / "Computers are piling up: lower the price or wait for word of mouth." /
  "Making and selling are even." That is the whole lesson, in the place you are looking.
- **Word of mouth is introduced by itself:** the "+N from word of mouth" part is reserved (blank) until it first exceeds
  1 a second, then appears with one ledger line ("Your first owner shows a friend. The friend wants one."). It is bold,
  so the eye goes to the compounding part.
- **Counters stay two:** You have (operations) and Money. Users lives in the panel, not as a third counter (three equal
  counters would squeeze 1024 px laptops and phones). The speed line under "You have" names the source once users
  dominate: "+2.1 million per second (from 4,120 users)".
- **Phone:** the switch wraps like Stage 2's; the panel lines wrap; nothing is wider than the column.
- **Bot-friendly:** one setting with three values; the sim can try each (rule 8).

## 3. The handover from Stage 2 (in place, ~2.6 s, as in 1946)
- "Pass the ledger on" → palette cross-fades (0.6 s) to beige/amber; the stripe fades in under the header.
- Ledger: divider "Stage 3 · The garage", then Stage 2's last word (the 4004 advert), then the opening line.
- Header: date "Nov 1971", "You are: **Garage founder**" (underlined 3 s, as the 1946 role).
- **Goal bar:** the new stage goal only, IN TOTAL kind, 0 %: "Run N operations in total to put a computer in every home".
- **You have:** the operations carry over untouched (the number never resets; same place, same size).
- **Stage 2's money: reset, said plainly.** The lab's dollars were the lab's. The Money counter stays exactly where it was
  and drops to the founder's savings (a small fixed sum, systems to set), with one ledger line:
  "You leave the division with a pension, a soldering iron and some savings." Carrying millions would let you skip the
  stage; a vanishing Money counter would make "You have" jump to the centre and back. Keeping the slot avoids both.
  (Fix for both stages: `has-market` stages reserve the Money cell from the first frame; today `#balances.two` toggles
  when selling opens, which moves "You have" mid-stage.)
- **Legacy row:** one fixed row, "The computing lab, 1946–71 · Legacy" / "26.1 million operations per second, forever."
  (It already includes Bletchley; no second row.) Slides in as in 1946.
- Producers, cards and the Sell panel of Stage 2 are forgotten; "Your garage" rebuilds with the Legacy row on top.

## 6. Preferences checklist
Concise, plain, high contrast (all AA+, text AAA), emphasis by **bold** never size, no pop-ups, no confirmations,
nothing flickering (forecast and status lines change at most once a second, rounded to 2 significant digits; no
blinking cursor despite the terminal look), locked things say why, goal bar = stage goal only (then the 2 finale
steps), layout never jumps (Money cell kept across the handover, word-of-mouth slot reserved, status line one fixed line).

## 7. Risks
- Three numbers in one panel (price forecast, users, status) is the limit; anything more goes to the ledger.
- If systems choose a model where price barely matters, the switch becomes Stage 2 again; the three status texts must
  each be reachable in normal play.
- "Operations on other people's computers count as yours" needs one ledger line at the first sale to make it fair.
